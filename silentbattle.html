A Silent Battle

Agony, Responsibility, and Resolve

It was an unforgettable turning point in my life. As I stepped into my twenties, an unknown enthusiasm surged within me. "I am an adult now, I am independent," I told myself with pride. I firmly believed that as a citizen, I possessed many rights, and those rights would grant me freedom and autonomy. I felt that the power to shape my life with my own hands, according to my own desires, had finally come to me. I hoped that every step I took in this new phase would bring a new responsibility and a new sense of dignity.

At that moment, I felt—now I can explore further, spread my wings and fly as high as the sky, and learn so many new things. But this wasn't just about growing older. It was a yearning to turn my past experiences into lessons and shape my future into something better. I decided that I would never become a slave to habits—especially alcohol, smoking, and drugs. I have always loathed them. Because, my father drowned himself in those very addictions and brought our lives to this state.

He did not give me a good education, he did not share great knowledge, he didn't even provide wealth. But, he gave me life. That is why I must plant this word firmly in my heart—'Because he didn't care for his children, I grew up like this... but I will not ruin the lives of my children.'

If I do that, the sacrifice my mother made would go in vain. She stood like an unwavering pillar for our family. Even when no one supported her... she gave up her friends, her relatives, even the smallest of entertainment, and worked tirelessly day and night for me. She endured the bitter words spoken by neighbors, relatives, and even her own family. That is why I must make her life better than it has ever been. I must answer everyone who insulted her through our success and our dignity.

The days that began like that suddenly started turning upside down. The discussions between my brain and my heart began to erupt like a slow volcano. Those days turned darker than I had ever imagined. It was then I realized—this was the first mistake I made as an adult. But my heart gave me courage—'We are human, mistakes are natural. Don't worry, move forward.' Those words became my strength. I pushed myself and moved on. With the resolve that my children should not grow up in hardships like I did, I joined college for my degree. It felt like heaven. The thought of becoming a graduate in a course I loved filled my heart with joy.

But life moves like a graph—always in unpredictable ways.

I am twenty years old, yet I am still dependent on my mother. I am unable to achieve what I want without her help. My elder brother did not support her financially, and my father never did from the beginning. Am I doing the same thing now? Is this the character I have learned all these years! This is the biggest 'Mahardasha' (great phase) of my life. A rage and anger I had never felt before erupted against myself. A great turning point stood before me in this lesson called life. There were only two paths—one, to study no matter how difficult; the other, to work. Because I resolved that from now on, my mother should not work. My life seemed to come to a standstill right there. It felt as if a massive mountain had been placed on my shoulders. A pain I had never seen, a burden I had never experienced, I was carrying now.

At that very moment, I made another mistake. I stubbornly decided to study while helping her. But in the process of chasing my goal, I realized I had stepped onto two boats at once. In my attempt to stand by her, I began to notice the terrifying agony and the hardships she was facing behind her smile. Gradually, I began to fail at balancing both. I couldn't help her properly on one side, and on the other, I was losing focus on my studies, gripped by the fear that I was pushing her into even newer troubles.

Even though I was losing myself, I moved forward keeping my mother's words in my heart. The surroundings, the friends, the family members... sometimes the self-loathing I felt wounded me many times. Yet, I complained to no one. In a place where there was no path to learn, my heart showed a way—"Learn the lesson through pain." That's when I started punishing myself. I sought to increase my mental fortitude through physical pain by hurting my body. My physical strength waned, but a stubbornness grew inside me. That was another huge mistake I made. When I took that decision, I never imagined it would crush me this much.

It was during this time that the horrific incident happened which shook my soul.

My father met with a terrible accident. There was blood and tears everywhere. But strangely... even though my father was between life and death, there was no movement in me. While people were running away in fear seeing the flow of blood, I took him in my arms and ran hundreds of meters to the clinic. I felt no emotion, I just did it as a duty. But then a scene occurred that burnt me alive. I saw tears in my mother's eyes. The whole world around was crying, but the tear that came from her eyes killed me.

Suddenly my eyes began to close, and I felt like I was losing consciousness. A conflict began between my brain and my heart, as if I were falling down the stairs. My brain questioned me—"Hey, your father was bleeding to death before your eyes and you felt nothing, you ran carrying him for hundreds of meters. But why did all your strength vanish the moment you saw a tear in her eyes? Why did your functioning stop?" At that moment, I realized that my mother's tears hurt me more than my father's death.

It was in this long struggle that I realized the true colors of this society. People here do not respect your personality; they calculate how much you earn and what your 'Cost' is. I understood that the respect given to you changes based on what you possess.

Coming to the matter of the Divine, the dilemma of whether God exists or not was always within me. To tell the truth, I do not believe God exists in a single form, which is why I never climbed temple steps or prayed to anyone. I am a person beyond religion and caste (Anti-religion & Anti-caste). I love people not based on their status, but based on the goodness within them. I respect the truth of 'what' they are rather than 'who' they are.

I follow some traditions out of respect, but not out of devotion. To me, the Divine is not someone sitting in an idol; I believe this entire creation is a reflection of Him. When every atom is created by Him, He is everywhere. That is why I never went anywhere searching for that Divine. The truth spoken by my conscience is my God. Every wound in this world taught me a lesson, every insult filled me with a stubbornness.

Finally, my father recovered from that accident. But misfortune knocked on our door in another form. The mistakes my elder brother made and the wrong decisions he took pushed us into unexpected troubles. I, who wanted to live as a respectable family, and my mother... finally had to face the situation of climbing the steps of a police station. Even as all this is happening, we are still fighting in this whirlpool of hardships. In this journey, my mistakes became my teachers, and my friendships became my support. This journey is not for perfection, but for growth. Every stumble in every step made me stronger, every small victory increased my confidence. Whenever I lose focus on my goal, I look at the scars on my body. That physical pain pushes me back onto the right path.

Let's see... to which shore my life leads me from here!
