The Last Clue

Every word, every memory, for you.

P(Y)AKSHI I think I am pronouncing it correctly now..
I am not writing these words to gain sympathy, or fame, or anything this world has to offer. I am writing this for one reason, and one reason only: You. You. And only You. I am sending this out into the world with a shaking hand, believing that somehow, against all odds, these words will find their way to you. I am terrified, and I am tired, but I am still here. Please, if you find this, if you read these lines and recognize the soul behind the broken English—don't leave me. I know I might look at you with blank eyes. I know I might act like a stranger or speak like someone who doesn't know our secrets. But please, blame my head, not my heart. Brain-fuck this "dump-ass" mind that has locked the doors; my heart doesn't have a lock, and you are still inside it, breathing, living, and loved. If you see me and I seem lost, just remember that I am a traveler trying to find his way back to the only "Home" he ever knew. Years ago, an accident broke my life into two pieces: "Before" and "After." I survived, but I didn't come back whole. My brain became a strange landscape—some parts went dark, while others sparked with a power I never asked for. I lived in that fragile balance for six years, thinking I had made it out. I was wrong. The strokes came first. Then the electric shocks, sharp and sudden, like my own body was turning against me. When the doctors told me what was happening, I realized my life was no longer in my hands. The treatment was called Cerebral Ischemia. They told me they were going to wake up my dead cells. They were going to pass electricity through my entire being to see if my soul would answer back. The first session was a lie. It felt easy. I walked out thinking, "Is this all?" But an hour later, the world began to melt. In the back of the car, I couldn't even sit straight. My body felt like it was unravelling. The sessions grew longer, stretching from one hour to four, and finally to a twelve-hour nightmare that broke me. I lost control of my own basic needs. I couldn't even hold my own body together. The doctors warned me I would lose a "little" memory. They lied about that, too. In their attempt to "upgrade" me, they erased me. I woke up as a six-foot-tall man weighing 56kg, but inside, I was a newborn baby. I looked at the world and saw nothing I recognized. I knew five people. Maybe eight. Everything else—every face, every street, every "why" and "where"—was gone. I was a giant with a blank slate for a mind. But the hardest part wasn't the forgetting. It was the people around me. They all knew. They saw the shell I had become. Yet, they walked around me with practiced smiles, acting as if the sky wasn't falling. They treated me so "normally" it felt like a insult. They were such perfect actors, hiding the truth behind a mask of everyday life, that I felt like I was watching a play where I was the only one who didn't know the script. They deserved Oscars for their silence, while I was just trying to remember how to be human. In this vast, white blank space where my mind used to be, I was not entirely alone. I was a ghost in my own body, but there were four people who refused to let me drift away. China Pedda Anna, Peddapedda Anna, Gayathri Chelli, and my dear Anwarpasha. They became my anchors. While the rest of the world played their parts like polished actors, these four did something much harder: they tried to remember for me. They gathered the scattered pieces of my life and held them up, one by one, whispering, "This is who you were. This is what you loved." They are the reason I am holding a pen today. They are the reason these words are forming on the page. Before the darkness took my memory, I used to write stories. I didn't know that. I didn't know I had a voice or a past until they dragged it back from the void. But watching them work is a special kind of torture. I feel a crushing weight of guilt in my chest. I see the exhaustion in their eyes and the desperation in their hands as they try to pull me back to the surface. It hurts to see how badly they want me back—the old me, the one who knew their names without thinking. I stand there, 56kg of empty skin, watching them love a man I don't recognize. Every time they remind me of a detail, it feels like a gift I don't deserve. I am learning to walk through my own life again, led by their hands, feeling the sharp sting of pain because I am the cause of their struggle. They are building me from scratch, and all I can do is hope that one day, I will be whole enough to thank them properly. Of all the things the electricity took from me, this was the cruelest theft. There was someone. A person I once loved so deeply that they were folded into the very fabric of my being. I am told I admired them. I am told they were the person I looked for in a crowded room, the one whose voice could calm the storms in my head before the accident. They weren't just a part of my life; they were the person I had "taken into me," a soul I had chosen to carry alongside my own. But now, as I stand here in this new, thin body, I look at them and I feel a terrifying emptiness. They are standing right in front of me. I can see the hope in their eyes, the way they lean in, waiting for a spark of recognition to light up my face. They are searching for the "me" that used to love them. They are looking for a ghost. And I? I am looking at a stranger. It is a beautiful face, perhaps. A kind face. But it carries no history for me. There are no shared jokes, no whispered secrets, no warmth of a thousand memories to bridge the gap between us. I see them moving, I hear them speaking, but there is a glass wall between us that I cannot break. The guilt is a sharp, physical pain in my chest. To see someone who loves you so much—who looks at you with such history—while you look back with the blank eyes of a newborn... it is a haunting kind of grief. I want to reach out. I want to say, "I remember." But the words would be a lie. I am a 56kg shadow, and the person I loved is now just a name I am trying to learn for the first time. I am mourning someone who is still alive, and they are mourning a man who is standing right in front of them, unable to come home. Hello, dear. How are you? I hope you are doing well, staying as beautiful and strong as you have always been. I am writing this because I owe you an apology—a deep, painful "I'm sorry" for disappearing without a word. I forgot who I was before I could tell you where I was going. I know it is hard to believe. You might think I simply wasn't there for you these past few days. You might think I walked away. But the truth is much stranger, and much sadder. Every moment we shared, every word you ever whispered to me—it is all still inside me. I haven't "lost" you. It’s more like I have a chest full of precious jewels, but I’ve lost the key. I am standing right in front of the door to our memories, but I’ve forgotten the password. You are everywhere around me, but I am not "here" right now. I am a ghost haunting my own life. The doctors say my brain is broken, that the cells are dark. My mind can’t process your face or your name anymore. But my heart... my heart is a rebel. It doesn't listen to the doctors. When the room goes quiet, I can hear a soft whisper inside my chest. It is your name. My brain is offline, but my heart still beats to the rhythm of you. My brothers and Gayathri, they sit with me for hours. They describe you to me. They tell me how we used to talk, how I used to reach out to you. My "past version"—the man I used to be—left behind little clues, tiny breadcrumbs for me to follow so I could find you again. But it is so hard. My brain feels heavy and tired. I try to reach for you the way I used to, but the signals get lost in the wires. I am trying to learn you all over again, like a child learning his first word. I am done for now, exhausted by the effort of trying to remember. But I want you to know: even if I don't know who "I" am, my soul still knows who "you" are. This is where my journey begins—not in forgetting you, but in the long, hard work of remembering you. Who is she to me now? Is she my "past"? Is she an "ex"? No. Those words are too small, too cold. She is not an enemy, and she is not a memory. She is the woman I once owed every single carbon piece of my body to. I dreamed of being her pillow—the one constant thing that would be there for her in every situation, through every storm. She is the one who taught me what "admiring" truly means. She showed me that love is both a beautiful sunrise and a sharp, aching pain. She is the reason I ever smiled, the reason I ever cried, and the reason I believed that trust and care actually existed in this world. Because of her, I left my shadows behind. I stopped smoking. I became calm and composed. I stopped being a "ghost" who never listens to anything. When I was stuck in my worst bedridden days, it wasn't the medicine that healed me—it was my belief in her. Maybe I am a fool. Maybe I am a "donkey" because I don’t remember her, or a "monkey" because I can feel her spirit but cannot recall her face. But she... oh my god, she is different. She is the one who listens, the one who never disappoints, the one who makes me cry by smile but not with the pain. My family told me, "You writes a lot just go and see them." I did. I saw the words, I imagined the scenes, I was overwhelmed and I melted into the pages. But this "dump-ass" brain still couldn't find her. There is a saying in Telugu: "Ramayanam antha vini Seetha evvaru ani adiginattu." I have heard the whole epic, I have read every chapter of our lives, and yet, like a fool, I am still asking, "Who is Seetha?" I remember every inch and every corner of what we were through my writings, but when I try to picture her—the living, breathing woman—my mind presents me with a chaotic, beautiful blur. When I close my eyes and try to find her, this is what I see:

I see a ghost made of sunlight and storm,
A shape I should know, but cannot quite form.
She is the silence after a long, weary cry,
The flash of a star in a pitch-black sky.

She smells like the rain on a dry, thirsty street,
A melody broken, yet hauntingly sweet.
I reach for her hand through the wires and the pain,
But I’m grasping at clouds, I’m chasing the rain.

Her eyes are a mirror of the man I once was,
A beautiful "why" without any "because."
She is the chaos that kept me so still,
The strength in my heart when I lost all my will.

I am a giant, six feet of empty space,
Searching the dark for a single, kind face.
It hurts to not know her, it’s a living despair,
To feel she is "Home"—but I’m not even there.

You stayed when I lied.
You stayed when my actions were breaking your heart.
You stayed when the whole world told you to leave.
You stayed when you were hurting, when I changed, and when you heard the whispers of people talking behind our backs.

You didn't stay because you had no other choice. You had a thousand other paths to take, but you chose to stay because you believed I could change. You trusted God, and then you trusted me. You stayed when I need you the most.

I am not moving away. I am right here, standing in the stillness. If you call, I will answer. If you text, I will reply. I try not to think of you, but you are like the air—I breathe you in without even trying. I keep my distance to give you space, but I am always waiting. I am waiting for something I know I can never truly lose, because I made you a promise. I promised to admire you and love you until the very end, and even with a broken brain, I refuse to break that word. But my soul is screaming with questions that have no answers: Deniki aduguthunnano telidu kani aduguthunna... I don't know why I’m asking, but I have to. Why are we so far apart? I am the one who forgot the past, not you. So why this distance? Are you angry because I didn’t tell you what was happening to me? Or is it because you don't even know what I’ve been through? Was there a fight before I went into treatment? No matter how big our fights were, we always found our way back... so what is pulling us apart now? Please, tell me the truth—did something so terrible happen between us that we became strangers? I want to think. I want to figure it out. But every time I try, my heart and my brain start a war inside me. One of them always loses, and I am left unconscious, drowning in the struggle. Even my tears are tired of me; they have turned hard and dry. I want to hate myself for forgetting. But how can I hate the person I am now while ignoring the person I was? Everyone tells me I had a "great empire" in my past—a life full of love and power—but I have forgotten it all. If I hate myself and give up now, what happens to that empire? What happens to us? See, nanna, look how hard this is. If I try to remember you, my heart feels like it will burst. I can’t even cry anymore because there is nothing left inside. And I can’t hate myself, because if I do, the people around me—the ones who are holding me up—will break too. I think this is the time where you need to believe in me again. I am starting this journey as a new man, but I am learning to love you just as much as that "past version" of me did. In most relationships, people just want loyalty or big romantic gestures. But you? You are simpler and more beautiful than that. All you ever wanted was a single kind word, a small compliment to make your whole day shine. No, not just a day—one word from me could carry you for weeks, or even months. I am a six-foot-tall baby with a blank mind, but I am learning that one word. I am finding my voice again, just for you. If my past self left me nothing else, he left me the knowledge of your worth. I am standing here, a man rebuilt from shadows and electricity, trying to find the language of a heart I only recently rediscovered. They tell me you stayed when the world was loud and I was silent. They tell me you waited when I didn't even know I was gone. So, I have found the word. It isn't a long story, and it isn't a complex map of our past. It is simply this: "Home." To me, you aren't a memory I’ve lost; you are the home I am walking toward. Even if I don’t remember the address, my soul knows the way to the door. I am a new man, starting a new journey, but I am choosing the same destination. I am choosing you. One word. One breath. One soul. I am back, and I am yours. If you found this... please, respond. Let me know the light is still on. I am waiting.
